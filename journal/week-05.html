The question

One sentence. It must be answerable, and it must have a "where" in it. This week, that is the sentence you wrote in class: at [site], [thing A] and [thing B] go together, because of [mechanism].

The data

Catchment: Global Watersheds (M. Heberger, mghydro.com), queried 13 October 2026. The main run used MERIT-Hydro (2021) at Lower precision. I compared it with MERIT-Hydro at Higher precision, HydroSHEDS (2013) and USGS (USA only). Unit: km² upstream of a snapped outlet.

Index: WRI Aqueduct 4.0 Water Risk, Baseline Annual and Baseline Monthly sublayers, via ArcGIS Living Atlas. CC BY 4.0, credited as “Aqueduct 4.0, World Resources Institute”. The baseline period is 1979–2019 and the data vintage is April 2023, so the figure describes a window that closed seven years ago. Unit: a HydroBASINS level-6 sub-basin, cut along state lines.

The method

Every run used the same input coordinate, 40.713, -74.004.

MERIT-Hydro snapped the outlet to 40.741, -74.008, about 3.1 km north on the Hudson near 14th Street. It returned 2 km² and reported “No river reaches were found.” My site lies outside this polygon. Higher precision returned the same 2 km².

HydroSHEDS returned 59 km². Its message gives my own coordinate as the outlet, but the map draws the snapped point near [40.79, -73.93] by the Harlem River, roughly [10 km] away.

USGS returned no boundary.

Aqueduct places my site in North Atlantic Coast Basin, New York (Pfafstetter 731300). That polygon is 57.93 km², about 580 times my site if the site is taken as 0.1 km². The adjacent polygon, also in New York, is 176.12 km².

What I found

Wastewater from my block crosses the divide. MERIT sends rain west to the Hudson, but the sewer sends it east. Lower Manhattan’s combined sewers converge at the Thirteenth Street Pumping Station on Avenue D. From there the flow is pumped under the East River to the Newtown Creek plant in Brooklyn (source: NYC DEP).

The three delineations did not agree: 2 km², 59 km², and nothing, a spread of about 30×. The datasets are not disagreeing about a ridge. They disagree about which water body I meant: MERIT chose the Hudson, and HydroSHEDS chose the Harlem River. HydroSHEDS’s polygon runs from the Battery into the Bronx, straight across the Harlem River. On flat, engineered, coastal ground, the dataset decides the answer.

The index barely moves. Annual stress is 0.06. Monthly values range from 0.030 in April to 0.088 in October, a factor of 2.9, but never cross the 0.1 class boundary, so the annual figure hides nothing here. The adjacent polygon reads 0.03, so the line between the two polygons is a hydrological boundary rather than an administrative cut.

What this does not show

I am using Aqueduct’s baseline water stress for my basin to stand for whether water at my site is in short supply. It might fail because the number describes the small coastal basin around Manhattan, but my tap water does not come from that basin. It arrives by tunnel from reservoirs more than 100 km upstate. A “Low” reading here says nothing about whether those distant reservoirs are under pressure.
