Joining Trade Data to the World Map

The question

What does the United States actually sell, where does it go, and does any of it trace back to that ground?

The data

Table. The Growth Lab at Harvard University, Atlas of Economic Complexity v24.4.1. United States, exports by destination, 2024, HS92, current US dollars, gross value. Goods data come from UN Comtrade (last retrieved 15/12/2025) and are reconciled from exporter and importer reports by the Growth Lab. It is a modelled estimate, not raw customs records. The file has 221 rows.

Boundaries. Natural Earth, Admin 0 – Countries, 1:110m, v5.1.1, 177 polygons, 168 attributes.

The method

I built a joined view layer in ArcGIS Online, which consumes no credits. Relationship one-to-one, left join, so that unmatched countries stay visible. Target field: ADMIN (Natural Earth). Join field: country (Atlas). This is a join on names, not codes.

Polygons with no value: 13 of 177.

Six are join failures. The data exist under a different spelling: Turkey/Turkiye, Ivory Coast/Côte d’Ivoire, United Republic of Tanzania/Tanzania, Republic of Serbia/Serbia, East Timor/Timor-Leste, Gambia/The Gambia.

Seven have no row at all. The United States itself; Puerto Rico, which is inside the US customs territory, so shipments there are not exports; Antarctica; Western Sahara; and Kosovo, Somaliland and Northern Cyprus. Those last three have -99 in both ISO_A3 and ISO_A3_EH, because no ISO 3166 code exists for them.

The evidence

What I found

A connection that holds. The largest US export is business services ($403B), but my choropleth shows goods only, so for the points I used the largest classified good: refined petroleum ($116B), with crude almost tied at $115B. This traces to a physical opportunity. The Permian Basin supplies the crude, and a deep-water Gulf coast lets refineries sit directly on shipping channels, so production and export happen in the same place. All three refineries lie within about 420 km of one coastline, in a country of roughly 9.8 million km².

A connection that breaks. By product the US exported $3.2T; by destination only $2T. The $1.2T gap is mostly services: business $403B, insurance and finance $223B, travel $214B, unspecified $211B, transport $102B, together about $1.15T, more than a third of everything the US sells. None of it comes out of the ground. Another $197B of goods is “commodities not specified”, larger than any classified product and impossible to trace to any place. The US basket has a physical story only in its minority, which is exactly what Thursday predicted: the more developed the economy, the weaker the link between what the ground gives and what the country sells.

What the map says about distribution. The goods that do have destinations are concentrated by proximity, not by distance or need: Mexico ($334B) and Canada ($329B) take a third of the total, and the rest of the darkest class is China ($142B). A choropleth of a total mostly redraws neighbours and large economies.

What this does not show

Gross, not value added: A component that crosses the US–Mexico border several times is counted each time, which might inflate Mexico ($334B) and Canada ($329B) relative to what they actually consume.

Nothing about who is paid: The statistic says nothing about wages, ownership, or where the profit lands.
